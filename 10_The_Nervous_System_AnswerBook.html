Q68. An electrical nerve impulse travelling down the axon reaches the swollen axon terminal, causing it to release molecules of a chemical neurotransmitter into the synaptic gap. D. Descriptive Type

Q71. A neuron consists of a cell body (cyton/perikaryon) containing the nucleus and Nissl's granules; short, branched dendrites that carry impulses towards the cyton; and a single long axon (up to about a metre long) that carries impulses away from the cyton. Many axons are covered by a fatty, insulating myelin sheath (with gaps called nodes of Ranvier) and an outer neurilemma, and end in swollen axon terminals that release neurotransmitters at a synapse.

Official Answer

Q72. The nervous system is divided into the Central Nervous System (brain and spinal cord) and the Peripheral Nervous System (all the nerves outside the CNS). The peripheral nervous system comprises the 12 pairs of cranial nerves (arising from the brain) and the 31 pairs of spinal nerves (arising from the spinal cord); it is further functionally divided into the somatic system (voluntary, controlling skeletal muscle) and the autonomic system (involuntary, controlling internal organs, itself divided into sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions).

Official Answer

Q73. The spinal cord is a long, cylindrical structure running through the vertebral column, continuous with the medulla oblongata of the brain. In cross-section it shows a central, H- (or butterfly-) shaped mass of grey matter (containing cell bodies) surrounding a fluid-filled central canal, with white matter (myelinated axons) arranged around the outside. It is covered by the same three meninges as the brain and protected externally by the bony vertebral column.

Official Answer

Q74. Unconditioned (natural) reflexes are inborn, present from birth, and require no prior learning or experience — e.g. sneezing, blinking, the knee-jerk. Conditioned reflexes are not present at birth but are acquired/learned through repeated experience and involve the cerebral cortex, so they can be modified or lost over time — e.g. salivating at the sight of a favourite food, or applying cycle brakes on sighting an obstacle.

Official Answer
