Q10. A D.C. motor consists of: (1) Armature coil (ABCD) — a rectangular coil of insulated copper wire that rotates. (2) Strong permanent magnet (N and S poles) — provides the magnetic field. (3) Split ring commutator — two halves of a split copper ring, one attached to each end of the coil. (4) Carbon brushes — press against the commutator, connecting the coil to the external circuit.

Working: When direct current is passed through the coil via the brushes and commutator, the sides AB and CD of the coil experience forces in opposite directions (by Fleming's left-hand rule). This creates a couple (torque) that rotates the coil. After half a rotation, the commutator reverses the direction of current through the coil, so the torque continues to act in the same direction, maintaining continuous rotation.

Function of commutator: It reverses the direction of current through the coil after each half rotation, ensuring that the torque always acts in the same direction, enabling continuous rotation of the coil.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q11. Principle: Electromagnetic induction — when a coil rotates in a magnetic field, the magnetic flux linked with it changes continuously, inducing an alternating e.m.f.

Construction: (1) Armature coil — a rectangular coil ABCD mounted on an axle, free to rotate between the poles of a magnet. (2) Field magnets — strong permanent magnets (N and S poles) providing the magnetic field. (3) Slip rings — two conducting rings attached to the two ends of the armature coil; they rotate with the coil. (4) Carbon brushes — press against the slip rings, connected to the external circuit.

Working: As the coil rotates, the sides AB and CD cut through the magnetic field lines alternately. By Fleming's right-hand rule, e.m.f. is induced in each side alternately in different directions as the coil rotates. When the coil plane is parallel to the field, the sides cut field lines at maximum rate, giving maximum e.m.f. When the coil plane is perpendicular to the field, sides move parallel to field lines, giving zero e.m.f. After a full rotation, the cycle repeats, producing alternating current.

Frequency: The frequency of a.c. equals the number of complete rotations of the coil per second (n rotations per second = n Hz frequency).
