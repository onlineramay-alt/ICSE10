D. Descriptive Type Questions

Q30. Transpiration is 'necessary' because it creates the suction pull that drives the ascent of sap, helps distribute absorbed minerals throughout the plant, and cools the plant in hot weather through evaporative heat loss. It is an 'evil' because the same stomata that must stay open to let CO2 in for photosynthesis inevitably lose large quantities of water vapour as an unavoidable side-effect — often far more water than the plant strictly needs — forcing a continuous trade-off between gas exchange and water conservation.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q31. A cactus grows in a hot, dry (xerophytic) habitat where conserving water is critical. Reducing its broad leaves to narrow spines drastically cuts down the exposed transpiring surface area, minimising water loss; photosynthesis is then carried out instead by the fleshy, green, water-storing stem.
