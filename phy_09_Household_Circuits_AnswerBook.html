Q6. It is not advisable to use copper wire as a fuse wire because:
(i) Copper has a very high melting point — it will not melt quickly when excess current flows, so it fails to protect the circuit.
(ii) Copper has low resistance — it does not heat up sufficiently on excess current to act as a protective device.
As a result, using copper wire instead of proper fuse wire can lead to overheating of wires and appliances, causing fire or serious damage.

Q7. Advantage of parallel connection: Each appliance operates at the full mains voltage (220 V) and can be switched independently — failure of one does not affect others.
Characteristics of fuse wire: (i) High specific resistance (heats up quickly on excess current). (ii) Low melting point (melts quickly to break the circuit). (iii) Made of an alloy (lead and tin) to combine these properties.
Wire connected to metallic body: The earth wire (green/yellow-green) is connected to the metallic body of the appliance for safety.

Official Answer

Q8. Thick wires are used in high-power appliances because they have a larger cross-sectional area, giving them lower resistance. Since high-power appliances draw large currents, a thick wire minimises the heat produced (H = I²Rt) and prevents the wire from overheating, melting, or causing fire.
Device: MCB (Miniature Circuit Breaker) or fuse.

Official Answer

Q9. (a) 'Current rating of a fuse is 5 A' means the fuse wire can safely carry up to 5 A of current continuously. If the current exceeds 5 A, the wire heats up, melts, and breaks the circuit.
(b) The voltage is stepped up to 132 kV to reduce the current for long-distance transmission (since P = VI; higher V means lower I at same power). Lower current means less heat loss in the cables (H = I²Rt), making transmission more efficient and economical.

Long Answer Questions

Q31. In the ring system the cable leaves the main distribution box, runs round the rooms of the house passing every socket, and returns to the same box, so that each socket is joined to the supply by two separate paths. Advantages: the current to any socket divides between the two paths, so each conductor carries only about half the current and a thinner and cheaper cable may be used safely; and if the cable is broken at any one point, current can still reach every socket by the other path, so the sockets keep working. It also allows extra sockets to be added anywhere on the ring without laying a new cable back to the box.
