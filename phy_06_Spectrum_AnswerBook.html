Q6. Variation of Angle of Deviation with Angle of Incidence:

As the angle of incidence (i) increases from 0° to 90°, the angle of deviation (δ) first decreases, reaches a minimum value (called the angle of minimum deviation, δₘ), and then increases again.

Key features of the graph:
• At a very small angle of incidence, deviation is large.
• As i increases, δ decreases to a minimum (δₘ) — this is the minimum deviation position.
• Beyond the minimum, as i further increases towards 90°, δ increases again.
• The graph is a U-shaped (parabolic) curve with a clear minimum.
• For a given prism, each colour has its own δₘ — violet has the largest δₘ and red has the smallest.

At minimum deviation:
• The refracted ray inside the prism is parallel to the base.
• The angle of incidence = angle of emergence.

Q7. Given: Equilateral prism → A = 60°; angle of incidence i = 45°; minimum deviation position.

(a) At minimum deviation, the refracted ray inside the prism is parallel to the base, so:
Angle of refraction r = A/2 = 60°/2 = 30°

(b) At minimum deviation:
i = (A + δₘ)/2
⇒ 45° = (60° + δₘ)/2
⇒ 90° = 60° + δₘ
⇒ δₘ = 30°

(c) Refractive index:
μ = sin[(A + δₘ)/2] / sin(A/2)
= sin[(60° + 30°)/2] / sin(60°/2)
= sin 45° / sin 30°
= (1/√2) / (1/2)
= 2/√2
= √2 ≈ 1.41

The refractive index of the prism material is √2 ≈ 1.41.

Q8. Given: λ_red = 7000 Å = 7 × 10⁻⁷ m; λ_violet = 4000 Å = 4 × 10⁻⁷ m

Using f = c/λ (c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s):

f_red = 3 × 10⁸ / 7 × 10⁻⁷ = 4.29 × 10¹⁴ Hz
f_violet = 3 × 10⁸ / 4 × 10⁻⁷ = 7.5 × 10¹⁴ Hz

Ratio: f_violet / f_red = 7.5 / 4.29 ≈ 7/4 = 1.75

So violet light has a frequency 1.75 times greater than red light.

Energy per photon: E = hf, so higher frequency → higher energy.
Violet light has higher energy per photon than red light.
