Official Answer

Q6. (i) When a lens is placed in water, its focal length increases. This is because the difference between the refractive index of the lens material and the surrounding medium (water) is less than that between the lens and air. Reduced refractive contrast means weaker bending of light, hence a longer focal length.
(ii) A thin lens has a greater focal length than a thick lens. A thicker lens has a more curved surface and bends light more strongly, giving a shorter focal length.

D. Long Answer Questions

Q11. When an object AB is placed between O and F₁ of a converging lens:

Three standard rays from point A:
1. Ray parallel to the principal axis → after refraction, passes through F₂.
2. Ray through optical centre O → passes undeviated.
3. Ray directed towards F₁ → after refraction, emerges parallel to the principal axis.

The three refracted rays all diverge on the right side. When extended backwards, they appear to meet at A' on the left side (same side as the object), forming the virtual image A'B'.

Three characteristics of the image:
1. Virtual — formed by the apparent intersection of rays; cannot be obtained on a screen.
2. Erect — upright, same orientation as the object.
3. Magnified — larger than the object.

Device: Simple microscope (magnifying glass / reading glass).

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q12. (a) Ray diagram: Object AB placed at 2F₁.
• Ray from A parallel to axis → refracts through F₂.
• Ray from A through O → passes undeviated.
• The two refracted rays meet at A' at 2F₂.
• Image A'B' is formed at 2F₂ — real, inverted, same size as object.

(b) Change in image size with a lens of greater focal length:
If the focal length f is increased (with the object remaining at the same position), the object is now placed closer to the lens than 2F (i.e., the object is between F and 2F of the new lens). According to the image formation rules for a convex lens:
- Object between F and 2F → image beyond 2F on the other side.
- The image becomes larger (magnified) compared to the original same-size image.
Therefore, the size of the image increases (becomes magnified) when the lens is replaced with one of greater focal length.

Q19. Step 1: When image is same size as object, the object is at 2F (distance = 2f from lens).
So initial object distance u₁ = −2f, image distance v₁ = +2f

Step 2: Object moved 12 cm closer → new object distance u₂ = −(2f − 12)
New magnification m₂ = −3 (real, inverted, 3× magnified)

m₂ = v₂/u₂ → −3 = v₂/[−(2f − 12)] → v₂ = 3(2f − 12)

Step 3: Apply lens formula for the new position:
1/f = 1/v₂ − 1/u₂ = 1/[3(2f−12)] − 1/[−(2f−12)]
= 1/[3(2f−12)] + 1/(2f−12)
= [1 + 3]/[3(2f−12)]
= 4/[3(2f−12)]

Therefore: f × 4 = 3(2f − 12)
4f = 6f − 36
36 = 2f
f = 18 cm

The focal length of the convex lens = 18 cm.

Q31. The principal axis is the straight line passing through the two centres of curvature of the surfaces of the lens. The principal focus is the point on the principal axis at which a beam of light travelling parallel to the axis converges after refraction through a convex lens. The focal length is the distance between the optical centre and the principal focus. To estimate the focal length quickly, form a sharp image of a distant object such as a window on a screen; since rays from a distant object are practically parallel, the image is formed at the focus, so the distance between the lens and the screen gives the focal length.
