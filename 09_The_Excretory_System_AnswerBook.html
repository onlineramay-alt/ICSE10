Q56. Ultrafiltration is the filtration of blood plasma under high pressure through the walls of the glomerular capillaries into Bowman's capsule, allowing water, glucose, salts and nitrogenous wastes to pass through while retaining blood cells and large plasma proteins.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q57. In cholera, severe vomiting and diarrhoea cause extreme dehydration. To conserve the body's dwindling water supply, the kidneys reabsorb an unusually large amount of water (and, along with it, reabsorb more urea than normal) from the glomerular filtrate. This drastically reduces urine output and allows toxic urea to accumulate in the blood — a condition called uremia, which can be fatal.

Official Answer

Q58. Gout is a painful condition caused by the crystallisation of excess uric acid (which has low solubility in water) in the joints, especially of the toes and fingers, leading to inflammation and pain.

Official Answer

Q59. Kidney stones are hard deposits formed inside the kidney when excessive uric acid and salts (such as calcium oxalate) crystallise out of concentrated urine instead of remaining dissolved.

Official Answer

Q60. Dialysis works on the principle of diffusion of small molecules across a semi-permeable membrane. The patient's blood is passed through fine tubes made of a selectively permeable membrane, immersed in a dialysing fluid whose osmotic pressure equals that of normal blood. Small molecules such as urea, excess salts and other wastes diffuse out of the blood, across the membrane, into the dialysing fluid, while blood cells and large proteins are retained — purifying the blood before it is returned to the patient's vein.

Official Answer
