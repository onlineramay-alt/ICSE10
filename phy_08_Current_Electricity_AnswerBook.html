Q26. An ohmic conductor is one that obeys Ohm's law, so the current through it is directly proportional to the potential difference across it and its resistance stays constant. Its V-I graph is a straight line passing through the origin, and a metallic wire such as nichrome at constant temperature is an example. A non-ohmic conductor does not obey Ohm's law, so the ratio V/I is not constant and its resistance changes with the conditions. Its V-I graph is a curve and does not stay straight; a junction diode is an example, since it conducts freely in one direction and hardly at all in the other.

Q27. In a series arrangement the same current I flows through every resistor, and the total potential difference across the combination equals the sum of the potential differences across the separate resistors. If the resistors are R₁, R₂ and R₃, then V₁ = IR₁, V₂ = IR₂ and V₃ = IR₃. Since V = V₁ + V₂ + V₃, we have IR = IR₁ + IR₂ + IR₃, where R is the equivalent resistance. Dividing throughout by I gives R = R₁ + R₂ + R₃, so in series the equivalent resistance is the sum of the separate resistances and is always greater than the largest of them.
