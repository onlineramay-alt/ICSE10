Q32. The Calvin cycle (also called the Calvin-Benson cycle, or the photosynthetic carbon-reduction cycle) is the set of light-independent biochemical reactions, occurring in the stroma of the chloroplast, in which carbon dioxide is progressively reduced (using the ATP and NADPH produced in the light reaction) and fixed into carbohydrate, with the enzyme RuBisCO catalysing the initial fixation step.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q33. Blackman's Law of Limiting Factors (1905) states that when a physiological process (such as photosynthesis) depends on several separate factors, the rate of that process is limited by the factor which is nearest to (or at) its minimum value at that time — i.e. the 'limiting factor' — regardless of how favourable the other factors are; increasing the limiting factor increases the rate, until another factor in turn becomes limiting.

Model Answer (not an official mark scheme)

Q43. (a) The rate of oxygen bubble release is used as an indirect measure of the rate of photosynthesis. (b) The bubble rate would be expected to decrease as the lamp is moved further away, because light intensity falls off sharply with distance, and light intensity is a limiting factor for the rate of photosynthesis — less light reaching the plant means a slower light reaction and so fewer oxygen bubbles per minute. (c) A variable to keep constant: water temperature (or CO2 concentration) — so that light intensity is the only factor being varied. A substance to increase the bubble rate: sodium bicarbonate (or potassium bicarbonate), which supplies extra dissolved CO2 to the water.
